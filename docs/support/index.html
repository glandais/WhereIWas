Support — WhereIWas GPS Logger — iOS 17 or later

Support

WhereIWas is made by one person. The fastest route to a fix is the issue tracker; everything else worth knowing is below.

Getting started

Three permissions, then leave it alone.

The app is designed to be set up once and then forgotten. It has no start button you need to remember to press before a shift.

  1. Allow location, then choose Always. iOS asks for While-Using first and offers Always a little later — accept it when it appears, or set it in Settings. Background recording is impossible without it.
  2. Keep Precise Location on. With it off, iOS gives the app positions accurate to a kilometre or so. The app still records and flags it on the status screen, but the track is no longer useful.
  3. Allow Motion & Fitness. This is what saves the battery: it is how the app knows you are moving without asking the GPS.
  4. Turn tracking on. It stays on across relaunches, reboots and terminations until you turn it off.

While the receiver is running you will see the blue indicator in the status bar. That is deliberate — an app recording your position in the background should say so.

Troubleshooting

There is a gap in my track

Turn on the audit trail in Settings and repeat the conditions. Every gap has an entry explaining it: a fix rejected with the check that failed and the value that failed it, a decision to stop the receiver, or a permission change. If the gap starts with a reboot or with the app being swiped away, see the limits on the overview — those are iOS behaviours no app can avoid.

Nothing was recorded after I restarted my iPhone

Expected until you unlock the device once. iOS keeps app data encrypted until first unlock, so nothing can run before then. Recording resumes on your first movement afterwards.

The battery drain is higher than I expected

Check Motion & Fitness permission first — without it the app has to use GPS to detect movement, which is the expensive way to do it. Then check whether coarse updates while stationary are on: they make iOS far less likely to terminate the app, at a small constant cost. A shift with roughly five hours of movement should land near 40–50 % of a full charge.

Tracking stopped and did not come back

If the app was force-quit from the app switcher, iOS stops sending it background events entirely. Open the app once and recording resumes on its own.

My exported file will not open

GPX exports are plain GPX 1.1 with extra data in an extension namespace; tools that do not understand the extensions ignore them and read the track normally. If a file fails to open anywhere, send it with a bug report.

Contact

Reporting something.

For a bug, include what you expected, what happened, your iOS version and iPhone model. If it concerns a gap or a wrong position, an audit-trail export covering the period is worth more than any description.
